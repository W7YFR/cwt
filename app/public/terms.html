Last updated November 25, 2026

CWT is a free tool for practicing Morse code (CW). By using it you agree to these terms. They are short. Please read them.

1. What CWT does

CWT listens to you send Morse code through your microphone or another audio input. It times every dit, dah and gap, reads what you sent, and compares it with what you meant to send. It also plays generated QSOs for you to copy by ear. On the QSO page you work stations that CWT makes up, and they answer what you send.

2. How it reads your sending

CWT reads your sending from sound. Your microphone, your room, your key and your calibration all change what it hears. Every reading is an estimate: the letters it copies, your speed, your timing and your grades. They are a guide to your practice, not a measure of your skill.

3. The rag chew feature

The rag chew feature on the QSO page is optional. It is off until you enable it and explicitly accept these terms. Enabling it downloads a language model, Llama 3.1 8B Instruct by Meta, in a form prepared by the MLC project. The model is about 5 GB, and it comes from Hugging Face. The program that runs it, about 7 MB, comes from the MLC project on GitHub. CWT checks the program before it runs it. The browser keeps both on your computer until you remove them. The model runs on your computer's graphics card.

Once your exchange with a station is done, the model writes the station's lines. A language model writes them, not the author of CWT and not a real person. They can be wrong, made up, repeated, strange or offensive. The station, its operator and everything it says about itself are made up. Do not rely on anything it says, about radio, the rules or anything else.

Llama 3.1 is Meta's, under the Llama 3.1 Community License and its Acceptable Use Policy. When you download it, you agree to them. The rag chew feature is built with Llama.

The download uses about 5 GB of your data and your storage. Running the model works your graphics card, which can warm your computer and use up a laptop's battery faster. You can remove the model at any time from the QSO page's settings.

6. Privacy

Everything runs in your browser. There is no account, no upload and no server. Your recordings, what you key and your settings never leave your machine. They stay in the browser's own storage until you clear them. There are no analytics and no cookies.

CWT makes no network requests of its own, with one exception. When you enable the rag chew feature and explicitly accept these terms, it downloads the model and its program from the addresses in section 3. What you send to the model never leaves your computer. The page is blocked from sending anything anywhere else.
